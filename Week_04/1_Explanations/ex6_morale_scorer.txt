WEEK 4 - EXERCISE 6: THE MORALE SCORER (CASE STUDY)
===================================================
Solution code: 2_Solutions\ex6_morale_scorer.py

!! This exercise needs two data files from Brightspace:
!!     data\field_notes.txt     and     data\morale_words.txt
!! Put the "data" folder in the same folder as your .py file and run the
!! program from there. Otherwise you get:
!!     FileNotFoundError: [Errno 2] No such file or directory


WHAT THE QUESTION IS ASKING
---------------------------
This is the "sentiment analysis" idea from the lecture (the cafe reviews),
applied to penguin station notes.

field_notes.txt looks like this (first line is a header):
    colony,note
    North Ridge,generator broken again, crew tired and hungry
    Seal Point,...

morale_words.txt is a LEXICON: words with a score (good words are
positive, bad words are negative):
    tired,-0.6
    happy,0.7
    ...

Goal: give every note a score (add up the scores of the words in it),
then total the scores per colony and print:
    North Ridge: 2.4
    Seal Point: 3.8
    Windward: 1.7


FIRST WRITE THE PLAN (pseudo-code, 4 lines)
-------------------------------------------
  1. Load the lexicon into a dict   (word -> weight)
  2. Read the notes, skipping the header
  3. Score each note: add the weights of the words found in the lexicon
  4. Add each score to its colony's total, then print the totals


STEP BY STEP
------------
STEP 1 - lexicon into a dict
    lexicon = {}
    for line in open("data/morale_words.txt"):
        parts = line.strip().split(",")    # "tired,-0.6\n" -> ["tired", "-0.6"]
        lexicon[parts[0]] = float(parts[1])
  .strip() removes the invisible newline at the end of each line.
  float() is needed because everything read from a file is TEXT.

STEP 2 - skip the header
    notes_file = open("data/field_notes.txt")
    header = notes_file.readline()     # reads line 1 and moves the bookmark
    for line in notes_file:            # the loop now starts at line 2
  (This is the "bookmark" idea from Week 2 and Week 3.)

STEP 3 - split each line ONCE and score it
    colony, note = line.strip().split(",", 1)
  The ,1 means "split at the FIRST comma only". The note itself has commas
  ("broken again, crew tired"), and we do not want those split.
    score = 0.0
    for word in note.lower().split():  # lowercase, then cut into words
        if word in lexicon:
            score = score + lexicon[word]

STEP 4 - add to a per-colony total (THE COUNTING PATTERN, very common)
    if colony in totals:
        totals[colony] = totals[colony] + score
    else:
        totals[colony] = score         # first time we see this colony
  Then print, rounded to 1 decimal:
    for colony in totals:
        print(f"{colony}: {round(totals[colony], 1)}")


PART (a) - CHECK BY HAND
------------------------
"generator broken again, crew tired and hungry" should score -1.7.
Open morale_words.txt, find each word of the note that appears in it,
and add up their weights on paper. If your total is -1.7, your loop is
right. (Words that are not in the lexicon count as 0.)
Note: .split() keeps punctuation, so "again," with its comma does not
match "again". That is the expected behaviour for this sheet.


PART (b) - OPTIONAL
-------------------
Make your own mini lexicon. Label 5 notes as good or bad. Every word in a
good note gets +1, and every word in a bad note gets -1. Score everything
again and compare the colony ranking. The point: the labels are a human
judgement, and they change the results.


COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
* Forgetting float() -> the weights stay strings, and adding them gives
  TypeError (or joins them as text).
* Using split(",") on the note line -> too many pieces, and you get
  ValueError: too many values to unpack.
* Not skipping the header -> "colony" becomes a fake colony in totals.
* Forgetting .lower() -> "Tired" does not match "tired".
